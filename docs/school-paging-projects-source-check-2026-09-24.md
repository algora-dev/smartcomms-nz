# NZ school paging projects — source check

**Checked:** 24 September 2026  
**Applies to:** `/guides/nz-school-paging-projects`

## Editorial method

This SmartComms page is a **cross-project analysis**, not a republication of the source case studies.

- Project-specific facts are attributed to the public account that published them.
- SmartComms writes its own synthesis, buyer questions, lifecycle commentary and cross-project comparisons.
- Manufacturer/current-product sources are used separately from installer/project sources.
- Publication dates and project/completion dates are stored separately. A publisher's article date is not treated as an installation date unless the source says so.
- Publisher, named dealer and integrator roles are kept separate where the source provides those roles. No installer role is inferred merely because a company published the account.
- Amplifier-fed architecture, retained existing infrastructure and future equipment relocation are treated as different concepts. Ormiston's decoder-to-amplifier design is not labelled as reuse; Lincoln's future re-installation requirement is not evidence that existing speakers/cabling were retained.
- A project marked `Not stated` in the matrix is not being scored as incapable; the public project account simply did not establish that feature for the installation.
- SmartComms has not independently inspected the five installations.
- Third-party case-study photographs, diagrams and prose are not reproduced.
- No case-study publisher is described as a SmartComms partner unless that relationship is separately and accurately disclosed elsewhere.

## Date / role / architecture distinctions

- **Three Kings:** 1 March 2024 is the Edwards publication date. The public article does not separately state the installation date.
- **Ormiston:** 23 August 2017 is the Pacific AV publication date. Australasian Audio Engineering is explicitly named as the dealer. The project architecture uses network decoders feeding amplifier channels; that is not evidence of retained pre-existing speakers or cabling.
- **Lincoln:** NZAV states the project was implemented in 2024. The account describes a 120-zone system intended to be removed and re-installed in future replacement buildings. That is equipment-relocation planning, not an infrastructure-reuse claim.
- **Cotswold Mātāhae:** the page states **Completed: November 2025**. It explicitly says AV Integration designed and integrated the solution. This is the only project in the five-account set whose public account explicitly describes two-way classroom calling.
- **Thorndon:** CIE Group is the publisher and Pure Tech is explicitly named as integrator. The public page does not state a project completion or publication date.

## Official NZ emergency-planning context

Ministry of Education:

- Main guidance: https://www.education.govt.nz/education-professionals/schools-year-0-13/health-safety-and-wellbeing/prepare-emergency-or-traumatic-incident
- April 2026 guide: https://web-assets.education.govt.nz/s3fs-public/2026-08/Planning%20and%20preparing%20for%20emergencies%20310826.pdf

The main guidance states that the board must maintain and keep the emergency management plan up to date. The April 2026 guide includes effective internal emergency communications in the plan requirements and, in its section on alarms/signals for evacuation, lockdown or shelter in place, asks schools to consider distinct alert signals or warning systems that can be heard both inside and outside.

Used only for the policy/safety boundary and buyer-planning questions. SmartComms does not claim a particular paging product satisfies a statutory or Ministry-mandated emergency-system standard merely because it can play lockdown/emergency messages.
